# Destination: USA

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a B1/B2 Tourist Visa to enter the United States. The application process involves completing the DS-160 online form, paying the visa fee (approx. 185 USD), and attending both a biometrics appointment (OFC) and an in-person visa interview at a US Embassy or Consulate in India. Due to high demand, appointment wait times can be lengthy, so planning several months or up to a year in advance is crucial. Upon approval, Indian travelers typically receive a 10-year multiple-entry visa allowing stays of up to 6 months per entry.

## Category: Transport
The United States is a massive country best navigated by domestic flights (Delta, United, American, Southwest) for intercity travel between major metropolitan hubs. In dense cities with mature public transit like New York City, Chicago, Washington D.C., and San Francisco, subways and buses (with tap-to-pay using OMNY or Ventra) are the fastest way to get around. In sprawling cities like Los Angeles, Las Vegas, or Miami, or when visiting National Parks (Grand Canyon, Yellowstone, Yosemite), renting a car or using ride-hailing apps like Uber and Lyft is indispensable.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the United States Dollar (USD / $). Contactless credit cards, debit cards, and mobile wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay) are accepted almost everywhere, including street food trucks, subways, museums, and supermarkets. Tipping is a mandatory cultural and economic norm in the US hospitality industry: tip 18% to 22% in sit-down restaurants, 1 to 2 USD per drink at bars, 2 to 5 USD per bag for hotel bellhops, and 15% to 20% for taxi or ride-share drivers.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
American culture is diverse, individualistic, informal, and emphasizes friendliness and personal space. Smiling and saying 'Hi, how are you?' is standard when interacting with service staff and strangers. Pedestrians generally have right of way at marked crosswalks, but jaywalking can result in fines in certain jurisdictions. Be aware that listed retail and restaurant prices in the US do not include state and local sales taxes, which are added at the final checkout register.

## Category: Safety And Scams
The United States is generally safe for tourists, but travelers should remain aware of their surroundings in certain urban downtown pockets and near deserted metro stations late at night. Common tourist scams include unmetered livery cabs at airports (always use designated yellow cab stands or Uber/Lyft pickup zones) and aggressive costumed street performers demanding tips in places like Times Square in NYC and Hollywood Boulevard in LA. In national parks, observe wildlife safety distances strictly and carry bear spray where advised.
