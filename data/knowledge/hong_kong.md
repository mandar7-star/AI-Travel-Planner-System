# Destination: Hong Kong

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders can visit Hong Kong visa-free for up to 14 days provided they complete an online Pre-arrival Registration (PAR) before their trip. The PAR application is free of charge and usually approved instantly upon completing the online form on the Hong Kong Immigration Department website. Travelers must print the PAR notification slip on an A4 sheet and carry it alongside their passport valid for at least six months. If the PAR application is unsuccessful, a conventional visa must be applied for through the Chinese Embassy.

## Category: Transport
Hong Kong possesses one of the world's most sophisticated and accessible public transportation networks. The Mass Transit Railway (MTR) network reaches every major tourist precinct, while the double-decker trams (Ding Ding) offer an iconic way to explore Hong Kong Island cheaply. The Star Ferry provides scenic, budget-friendly harbor crossings between Kowloon and Central. Pick up an Octopus Card or use contactless credit cards on the MTR, buses, and ferries for effortless tap-and-go commuting.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Hong Kong Dollar (HKD), which is pegged to the US Dollar. International credit cards are accepted across shopping centers, hotels, and restaurants. The contactless Octopus Card is universally used not only for all transit modes but also for payments at convenience stores, bakeries, fast food chains, and vending machines. Cash is necessary for wet markets, traditional cha chaan teng tea cafes, and small street-food stalls in Mong Kok.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Hong Kong blends traditional Cantonese heritage with fast-paced international cosmopolitan culture. Efficiency, punctuality, and personal space are highly valued, so stand on the right side of escalators to allow others to walk on the left. In traditional restaurants, it is common practice to rinse your tableware with hot tea provided at the table before dining. Avoid loud speaking in public transit vehicles and keep queues orderly.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Hong Kong is one of the safest global cities with extremely low violent crime rates. Minor tourist scams include pushy vendors selling counterfeit luxury goods or tailor-made suits along Nathan Road in Tsim Sha Tsui. Always confirm the unit of measurement (per tael vs. per catty) when purchasing dried seafood or traditional Chinese herbal medicines to avoid unexpected high bills. Verify restaurant receipts for automatic 10% service charges and tea charges.
