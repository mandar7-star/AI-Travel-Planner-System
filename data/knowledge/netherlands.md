# Destination: Netherlands

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Schengen Short-Stay Visa to visit the Netherlands. Apply through VFS Global Netherlands in India with your travel documents, hotel reservations, return flight itinerary, minimum 30,000 EUR travel medical insurance, salary slips, 3 years ITR, and 6-month bank statements. Appointments should be booked at least 1 to 2 months prior to your intended departure date, especially during the spring Keukenhof tulip season. The visa processing time is typically 15 business days.

## Category: Transport
The Netherlands has a world-leading transit system operated by NS (railways) and municipal operators like GVB in Amsterdam. You can travel across the entire country by tapping in and tapping out using any contactless international credit/debit card on trains, trams, metros, and buses (OVpay). Amsterdam is also the bicycle capital of the world; renting a bicycle is a great way to explore, but always stay within designated red cycle lanes and yield to local cyclists.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Euro (EUR). The Netherlands is almost completely cashless, with many supermarkets (such as Albert Heijn), museums, parking meters, and restaurants displaying 'Pinnen alleen' (card payments only). Contactless Visa and Mastercard credit/debit cards are accepted universally. Carrying large amounts of physical cash is unnecessary and often inconvenient, though having 10-20 EUR for small flea markets like Waterlooplein is useful.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Dutch culture is characterized by egalitarianism, direct communication ('bespreekbaarheid'), and tolerance. English proficiency among locals is among the highest in non-native English speaking countries, making daily interactions effortless. When walking in cities, never stroll in designated red-paved cycle paths (fietspad), as cyclists have right of way and ride at high speeds. Tipping is not mandatory, though rounding up the bill or leaving 5-10% in sit-down restaurants is customary.

## Category: Safety And Scams
The Netherlands is safe and welcoming for solo travellers and families. The primary tourist concern is pickpocketing in crowded areas of Amsterdam, such as Dam Square, Amsterdam Centraal Station, and the Red Light District. Taking photos or videos of workers in the Red Light District windows is strictly prohibited and heavily enforced. Never rent unlicensed bicycles or purchase questionable electronics or drugs from street vendors.
