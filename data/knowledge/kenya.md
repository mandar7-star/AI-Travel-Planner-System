# Destination: Kenya

## Category: Visa
Kenya has abolished traditional visas and replaced them with the mandatory Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) system (etakenya.go.ke). All Indian travelers, including infants and children, must apply online for an approved Kenya eTA at least 3 days prior to departure. The eTA processing fee is approximately 32.50 USD per traveler. Required documents include a valid passport with at least six months validity, a clear passport photo, confirmed flight itinerary, hotel booking confirmations or safari lodge vouchers, and a valid Yellow Fever vaccination certificate for travelers arriving from or transiting through endemic areas.

## Category: Transport
Safari travel across Kenya's premier national parks (such as Masai Mara, Amboseli, and Lake Nakuru) is typically conducted in 4x4 Land Cruisers with pop-up roofs arranged through registered tour operators. For traveling between Nairobi and the coastal port of Mombasa, the Madaraka Express Standard Gauge Railway (SGR) provides a scenic and modern 4.5-hour train journey with views of Tsavo wildlife. In Nairobi, Uber and Little Cab are widely used and recommended for safe urban transit, while colorful 'matatu' minibuses operate commuter routes.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Kenyan Shilling (KES). Kenya is a global pioneer in mobile money via M-Pesa, which is used everywhere from upscale supermarkets to street fruit vendors (foreign visitors can register for M-Pesa with a local Safaricom SIM card). Major safari lodges, upscale hotels, and park entrance gates (via eCitizen portal) accept international Visa and Mastercard credit cards. Carrying cash in KES or crisp US Dollar bills (printed after 2013) is necessary for safari guide tips, village craft markets, and gratuities.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Kenyan culture is warm, community-driven, and characterized by the famous Swahili greeting 'Jambo' and the philosophy of 'Hakuna Matata' (no worries) and 'Harambee' (pulling together). Respect local tribal customs when visiting traditional Maasai or Samburu cultural bomas, and always obtain permission before taking photographs of individuals. Note that Kenya has strict environmental laws: single-use plastic carrier bags and single-use plastic water bottles inside protected national parks and reserves are strictly banned.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Safari game reserves and tourist wildlife sanctuaries are peaceful and well-guarded by Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) rangers. In urban centers like Nairobi (historically nicknamed 'Nairobbery'), practice sensible city safety: avoid walking at night, keep valuables discreet, do not wear expensive jewelry on busy streets, and always use Uber for evening transit. On safari game drives, follow your driver-guide's instructions strictly: never step out of the safari vehicle in unauthorized park zones and keep arms inside vehicles near wildlife predators.
