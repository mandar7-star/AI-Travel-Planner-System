# Destination: United Kingdom

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a UK Standard Visitor Visa to visit England, Scotland, Wales, or Northern Ireland for tourism. Applications must be completed online via the UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) website and followed by biometric enrollment at a VFS Global center in India. The visa fee is approximately 115 GBP for a 6-month multiple-entry tourist visa, with standard processing taking around 3 weeks (priority processing is available for an extra fee). Note that the UK is not part of the Schengen zone, so a separate visa is mandatory even if you hold a Schengen visa.

## Category: Transport
The United Kingdom has an extensive railway grid connecting London to Edinburgh, Manchester, Oxford, and Bath. In London, the Transport for London (TfL) network—comprising the London Underground (the Tube), Elizabeth line, Overground, and red double-decker buses—can be accessed simply by tapping any contactless international credit/debit card or Apple Pay/Google Pay with automatic daily fare capping. Black cabs are metered and iconic, while Uber and Bolt operate reliably across major cities.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the British Pound Sterling (GBP / £). The UK is one of the most cashless societies in Europe, with contactless card payments accepted everywhere from street food markets (like Borough Market) to roadside parking meters. Many central London shops, restaurants, and bars operate strictly cashless policies and do not accept physical currency notes. Carrying more than 20-30 GBP in physical cash is rarely necessary.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Politeness, personal space, and queuing etiquette are deeply ingrained in British society. Always stand on the right side of escalators on the London Underground to let people in a hurry pass on the left. In British pubs, orders are placed and paid for directly at the bar counter rather than from the table. Saying 'please', 'thank you', and 'sorry' frequently is standard conversational manners.

## Category: Safety And Scams
The UK is generally very safe for tourists, though urban centers have experienced an increase in moped and e-bike snatching of smartphones. Avoid walking near curbs with your smartphone held out loosely in hand, especially around busy central London streets like Oxford Street, Soho, and Westminster. Be cautious of street gambling scams (three-card monte or shell games) on pedestrian bridges across the Thames.
