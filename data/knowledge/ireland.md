# Destination: Ireland

## Category: Visa
Indian citizens require an Irish Short-Stay 'C' Tourist Visa to visit the Republic of Ireland. Ireland is not part of the Schengen zone, meaning a Schengen visa is not valid for entering Ireland; however, Indian nationals holding an eligible UK Standard Visitor Visa (short-stay 'C') may visit Ireland visa-free under the British-Irish Visa Scheme (BIVS) provided they enter the UK first. Direct visa applications must be completed online via AVATS followed by document submission at VFS Global in India, with processing taking approximately 3 to 5 weeks.

## Category: Transport
In Dublin, public transport includes the Luas tram network, DART coastal electric rail, and Dublin Bus double-decker fleet; purchasing a Leap Visitor Card provides unlimited travel and substantial fare savings. For traveling across Ireland to the Cliffs of Moher, Galway, Cork, or Killarney, Iarnród Éireann (Irish Rail) and Expressway coach services provide reliable connectivity. Renting a car is popular for driving scenic coastal routes like the Wild Atlantic Way and the Ring of Kerry (note that driving is on the left side of the road).

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency in the Republic of Ireland is the Euro (EUR), while Northern Ireland uses the British Pound (GBP). Contactless credit and debit cards, Apple Pay, and Google Pay are accepted virtually everywhere across Ireland, from rural village pubs to city museums. Carrying a modest amount of physical cash (20 to 40 EUR) is useful for small tips, street musicians (buskers), and rural countryside admission booths.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Irish people are famously friendly, chatty, and welcoming, with a culture deeply centered on storytelling, live folk music, and social connection ('the craic'). In traditional pubs, the 'rounds system' is customary: if someone in your group buys you a pint, you are expected to buy a drink for the group in a subsequent round. Avoid discussing sensitive political or sectarian history, and respect standard queuing etiquette.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Ireland is an exceptionally safe travel destination with very low rates of serious crime. Urban centers like central Dublin (particularly north of the River Liffey and around Temple Bar late at night) require basic awareness against pickpockets and rowdy nightlife groups. When driving on narrow rural country roads, drive cautiously around tight blind corners and yield to oncoming vehicles at designated passing bays.
