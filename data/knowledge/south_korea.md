# Destination: South Korea

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a tourist visa (C-3-9) to enter South Korea, which must be applied for through Korea Visa Application Centers (KVAC) in India. Documents required include passport valid for six months, detailed travel itinerary, hotel confirmations, round-trip flight bookings, 6-month bank statements, and income tax returns (ITR). Processing time is generally 5 to 10 working days, and visa fees are approximately 40 USD plus service charges. Indian nationals holding valid visas for the US, Canada, Australia, or New Zealand who are in transit through South Korea to/from those countries can enter visa-free for up to 30 days under specific transit programs.

## Category: Transport
South Korea boasts a world-class public transportation system featuring the KTX bullet train network connecting Seoul to Busan, Daegu, and Gwangju in under three hours. In Seoul and Busan, the subway system is fast, clean, and fully bilingual with English and Korean signage. Purchase a T-money card or WOWPASS at convenience stores or airport kiosks for easy tap-in payments on subways, city buses, and taxis. Use Kakao T or Uber for reliable taxi hailing, and navigate with Naver Map or KakaoMap rather than Google Maps, which has limited transit routing due to domestic mapping regulations.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the South Korean Won (KRW). South Korea is virtually cashless, with credit and debit cards accepted everywhere from large departmental stores to small convenience stores and street kiosks. The WOWPASS prepaid card is popular among foreign tourists as an all-in-one payment card and T-money transport card that can be reloaded using multiple foreign currencies. Having a small amount of cash (10,000 to 50,000 KRW) is helpful for traditional food markets like Gwangjang Market or street cart vendors in Myeongdong.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Respect for elders and social hierarchy is central to Korean etiquette. Always receive and give items, payment cards, or beverages using both hands or with your right hand supported at the forearm by your left hand. Remove your shoes when entering traditional restaurants with floor seating (ondol), guesthouses, and homes. Wait for the eldest person at the dining table to begin eating before you start, and avoid sticking chopsticks upright into a bowl of rice.

## Category: Safety And Scams
South Korea is exceptionally safe at all hours of the day and night with minimal violent crime. Occasional tourist traps include overpriced cosmetics without listed prices in busy tourist shopping strips and unofficial taxis operating without meters at late-night entertainment districts. Always ensure the taxi meter is running or book through Kakao T to guarantee standard regulated fares.
