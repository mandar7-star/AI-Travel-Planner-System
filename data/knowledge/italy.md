# Destination: Italy

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Schengen Visa (Uniform Schengen Type C) to visit Italy. Applications must be submitted through VFS Global Italy up to six months before travel, but at least 3 to 4 weeks in advance during peak summer seasons. Required documents include a valid passport, Schengen travel medical insurance covering minimum 30,000 EUR, confirmed round-trip flights, hotel vouchers, 6-month bank statements showing financial stability, 3 years ITR, and an employment NOC. The standard Schengen visa fee is approx. 90 EUR plus VFS service fees.

## Category: Transport
Italy has a high-speed rail network operated by Trenitalia (Frecciarossa) and Italo, connecting Rome, Florence, Venice, Milan, and Naples seamlessly in 1 to 3 hours. Always validate paper regional train and bus tickets at yellow/green stamping machines before boarding to avoid steep fines. In Rome and Milan, the metro and tram networks cover major tourist sights efficiently. In Venice, transportation is strictly pedestrian or waterborne via Vaporetto water buses and private water taxis.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Euro (EUR). Contactless credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are legally accepted for transactions of all sizes, from espresso at local coffee bars to museum admissions. However, having small Euro coins and notes (5, 10, 20 EUR) is advisable for public restroom fees, city tourist tax (tassa di soggiorno) paid directly at hotels, and tipping. Bank ATMs (Bancomat) attached to major banks like Intesa Sanpaolo or UniCredit offer the best foreign exchange withdrawal rates.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Italian culture is deeply intertwined with art, gastronomy, and religion. When visiting churches like St. Peter's Basilica or Milan's Duomo, adhere to the strict dress code covering shoulders and knees (no tank tops or short shorts). Italians drink cappuccino only in the morning; ordering milk-heavy coffee drinks after 11 AM or during meals is viewed as unusual. A nominal 'coperto' (cover charge) is commonly added to sit-down dining bills in trattorias.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Petty theft and pickpocketing are prevalent around crowded tourist hotspots like the Colosseum, Rome Termini Station, the Duomo in Milan, and on crowded buses like Rome's Bus 64. Watch out for the friendship bracelet scam, fake gladiators requesting payment for photos, and unmetered airport taxis. Always choose official white city taxis with clearly posted municipal flat rates from Rome Fiumicino and Milan Malpensa airports.
