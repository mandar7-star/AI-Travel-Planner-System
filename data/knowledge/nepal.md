# Destination: Nepal

## Category: Visa
Indian citizens do not require a visa or passport to enter Nepal for tourism under the 1950 Indo-Nepal Treaty of Peace and Friendship. Indian nationals travelling by air must present either an original valid Indian Passport or a valid Voter Identity Card issued by the Election Commission of India. Aadhar cards, PAN cards, or driving licenses are generally not accepted as valid travel identification for air border clearance. No visa fee or entry permit fee is charged for Indian tourists.

## Category: Transport
Kathmandu's traffic can be chaotic and slow-moving during peak hours. Getting around urban areas is easiest using ride-sharing apps like Pathao and inDrive for affordable car and scooter rides. Domestic flights operated by Buddha Air or Yeti Airlines are the fastest way to travel from Kathmandu to Pokhara, Chitwan, or Everest trekking gateways like Lukla. For overland routes between Kathmandu and Pokhara, tourist deluxe AC buses offer comfortable daytime scenic journeys.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Nepalese Rupee (NPR), which is pegged to the Indian Rupee at a fixed rate of 1 INR = 1.60 NPR. Indian Rupee currency notes of INR 100 and smaller denominations are accepted practically everywhere across Nepal. High-denomination Indian currency notes (INR 200, 500) face restrictions under local regulations, so carrying INR 100 notes or withdrawing NPR directly from Nabil Bank or Himalayan Bank ATMs is advised. Cross-border UPI QR code payments are now widely supported across merchant stores in Nepal.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Nepal is a spiritually rich nation blending Hinduism and Buddhism. When visiting temples like Pashupatinath or stupas like Boudhanath and Swayambhunath, walk in a clockwise direction around shrines and prayer wheels. Remove leather items and footwear before entering sacred temple precincts, and note that non-Hindus are not permitted inside certain inner sanctums. Use the traditional 'Namaste' greeting with pressed palms to acknowledge locals with warmth.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Nepal is a very safe and hospitable country for travelers. Primary safety considerations involve mountain altitude sickness (AMS) on high-altitude Himalayan treks, for which gradual acclimatization is vital. Beware of fake trekking guides, unlicensed taxi drivers offering inflated rates at Tribhuvan International Airport, and over-priced pashmina or antique stores. Always hire certified trekking guides registered with the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal (TAAN).
