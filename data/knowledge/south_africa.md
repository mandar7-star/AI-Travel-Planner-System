# Destination: South Africa

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Tourist Visitor Visa to travel to South Africa. Applications can be submitted via the South African e-Visa portal (dha.gov.za) or physically through VFS Global South Africa in India. Required documents include a valid passport with at least two blank consecutive pages, confirmed return flights, certified accommodation itineraries, 3 months bank statements showing adequate funds, an employment leave letter, and tax documents. The standard processing time is approximately 10 to 15 business days, and the basic visa fee is waived for Indian tourist applicants (only VFS service fees apply for offline submissions).

## Category: Transport
South Africa is a vast country best explored by domestic flights for long distances (e.g., Safair, Airlink connecting Johannesburg, Cape Town, and Kruger Mpumalanga) or renting a car for scenic road trips like the Garden Route and Cape Peninsula. In Cape Town and Johannesburg, the MyCiTi bus and Gautrain high-speed airport rail offer reliable public transit connections. For general city transit and nightlife, Uber is widely available, secure, and the strongly recommended way to travel, especially after dark.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the South African Rand (ZAR / R). Credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are accepted across hotels, safari game lodges, shopping malls, fuel stations, and restaurants. In fact, many tourist attractions (like Table Mountain Cableway, Cape of Good Hope, and Kirstenbosch Gardens) operate cashless ticketing counters. Tipping is standard practice in restaurants (10-15%), for gas station pump attendants (R5 to R10), and for official parking marshals (R5 to R10 in cash).

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
South Africa, known as the 'Rainbow Nation', is a diverse society comprising 11 official languages and multicultural traditions. South Africans are generally outgoing, friendly, and enthusiastic about welcoming visitors. When visiting township cultural tours (such as Soweto in Johannesburg or Langa in Cape Town), always visit with an accredited local community guide. Greetings are important and conversations typically begin with a warm 'How are you?' or local slang like 'Howzit'.

## Category: Safety And Scams
While the country offers unforgettable landscapes and wildlife, personal safety awareness is paramount, particularly in major urban centers like Johannesburg and central Durban. Never walk alone at night, keep vehicle doors locked with windows rolled up while driving through city traffic, and do not leave valuables visible on car seats (avoid 'smash-and-grab' theft). When exploring Table Mountain or Lion's Head trails, hike only in daylight with groups, stay on marked routes, and use Uber for all evening transit.
