# Destination: Brazil

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Visit Visa (VIVIS) to travel to Brazil for tourism purposes. The application is initiated online through the Brazilian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (E-Consular portal) followed by submitting physical passport and supporting documentation to the Embassy of Brazil in New Delhi or the Consulate General in Mumbai. Key requirements include a passport with at least six months validity, confirmed return flight reservations, hotel vouchers or invitation letter, 3-6 months certified bank statements, and employment proof. The visa is typically issued for multiple entries with standard processing taking 10 to 15 working days.

## Category: Transport
Due to Brazil's vast continental dimensions, domestic flights (LATAM, Gol, Azul) are the primary way to travel between Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, Salvador, Foz do Iguaçu, and the Amazon (Manaus). Within Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo, the underground Metro systems are modern, air-conditioned, clean, and safe for tourists connecting key sights and beaches. For city rides, airport transfers, and late-night travel, ride-hailing apps like Uber and 99 are economical, reliable, and significantly safer than waving down street taxis.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Brazilian Real (BRL / R$). Brazil is an advanced digital payment economy where credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) and contactless payments are accepted by nearly everyone, from beachside caipirinha vendors in Copacabana to fine dining restaurants. While locals widely use the instant PIX QR payment system, foreign tourists rely on international credit cards. Carrying a small amount of cash (50 to 100 BRL) is useful for tips and small local markets; withdraw cash only from bank ATMs located inside secure branches (Banco do Brasil, Bradesco, Itaú).

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Brazilian culture is vibrant, expressive, and renowned for warm social hospitality, samba music, and football passion. A casual kiss on the cheek (one in São Paulo, two in Rio) or a warm handshake and hug is a standard friendly greeting. Portuguese is the national language; while English is spoken in international hotels and upscale tourism hubs, learning basic Portuguese phrases like 'Obrigado/a' (thank you) is warmly appreciated. Beachwear (bikinis, speedos, flip-flops) is strictly for the beach and should not be worn in city streets, churches, or restaurants.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Travelers should exercise elevated situational awareness in major urban areas like Rio de Janeiro and Salvador. Do not wear flash jewelry, gold chains, expensive watches, or openly carry expensive smartphones or DSLR cameras down busy public streets or on beaches (prevent opportunistic 'snatch-and-run' thefts). Stick to well-patrolled tourist areas (Copacabana, Ipanema, Leblon), avoid walking along beaches or poorly lit empty streets after dark, and always take an Uber for night journeys.
