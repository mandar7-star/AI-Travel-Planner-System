# Destination: Norway

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Schengen Short-Stay Visa to visit Norway for tourism. Applications are submitted through VFS Global Norway in India and require a valid passport, comprehensive Schengen travel medical insurance covering at least 30,000 EUR, confirmed flight and accommodation bookings, detailed itinerary, 3-6 months bank statements, and tax returns. Due to high demand during the summer fjord season and winter Northern Lights period, applying 4 to 8 weeks before departure is strongly recommended. The processing time is typically 15 working days.

## Category: Transport
Norway possesses an extensive and scenic public transportation network operated under the national journey planner Entur. High-speed Vy and SJ Norge trains connect Oslo to Bergen (the iconic Bergen Line) and Trondheim, while the world-famous Flåm Railway offers dramatic fjord vistas. To explore the western fjords (Geirangerfjord, Nærøyfjord) and Arctic regions (Tromsø, Lofoten Islands), Hurtigruten / Havila coastal express ships, car ferries, and Widerøe regional flights provide scenic connections. In Oslo and Bergen, public transit is fully electric, comprising metros (T-bane), trams, and electric ferries.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Norwegian Krone (NOK). Norway is virtually 100% cashless, with digital transactions and contactless credit/debit cards accepted everywhere, from luxury hotels to remote self-service hiking cabins (DNT) and street kiosks. Cash is rarely seen or accepted, and many establishments display signs stating 'Ikke kontanter' (no cash). Keep a chip-and-PIN enabled card ready for automated parking meters and unmanned electric vehicle charging stations.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Norwegian society is egalitarian, punctual, and guided by the cultural concept of 'Allemannsretten' (the universal Right to Roam), which allows anyone to hike and camp freely in open countryside as long as nature is respected and no trace is left behind. Punctuality is strictly observed for all appointments and tours. In saunas and fjord baths, customary hygiene rules require showering before entry and placing a towel beneath yourself on wooden benches.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Norway is exceptionally safe with negligible rates of crime and no prominent tourist scams. Safety risks are largely related to severe Arctic weather, icy winter pavements, and hiking in rugged terrain without proper gear. Always check Yr.no for local weather forecasts, pack layered waterproof clothing, and wear spiked ice grips (brodder) on shoes during winter in Tromsø and Oslo.
