# Destination: Mexico

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders holding a valid, unexpired multiple-entry visa or permanent residency from the United States, Canada, Japan, the United Kingdom, or any Schengen country can enter Mexico visa-free for tourism for up to 180 days. All arriving passengers must fill out the Forma Migratoria Múltiple (FMM tourist card) upon arrival. Indian citizens without a valid qualifying visa from the aforementioned countries must apply for a Mexican Tourist Visa in person at the Embassy of Mexico in New Delhi, which requires biometric appointment scheduling and proof of financial solvency.

## Category: Transport
For traveling between distant destinations in Mexico (such as Mexico City, Cancun, Oaxaca, and Los Cabos), domestic flights on carriers like Aeromexico, Volaris, and VivaAerobus are efficient and affordable. Within Mexico City, the extensive Metro and Metrobús rapid transit network is cost-effective, while Uber operates reliably, affordably, and safely across all major tourist cities. In the Yucatán Peninsula, the modern Maya Train (Tren Maya) and ADO first-class luxury buses provide comfortable overland connections between Cancun, Playa del Carmen, Tulum, and Merida.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Mexican Peso (MXN / $). Credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are accepted in hotels, upscale restaurants, supermarkets, and established beach clubs in Cancun and Mexico City. Cash is essential for street taco stalls, local handicraft markets, tipping, cenote entrances, and archaeological ruins. Use bank-affiliated ATMs (BBVA, Santander, Banorte) located inside secure bank branches or malls rather than freestanding street ATMs to avoid card skimming and high conversion fees.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Mexican culture is warm, family-oriented, and steeped in rich pre-Columbian indigenous and Spanish colonial heritage. Politeness is highly valued; greet service staff with 'Buenos días' (good morning) or 'Buenas tardes' (good afternoon) and say 'Por favor' (please) and 'Gracias' (thank you). Tipping ('propina') is an important social norm, standard at 10% to 15% in sit-down restaurants and 20 to 50 MXN per bag/day for hotel staff and tour guides.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Popular tourist regions like the Riviera Maya (Cancun, Tulum, Cozumel), Mexico City (Roma, Condesa, Polanco), Oaxaca, and Baja California Sur are generally safe when practicing standard travel vigilance. Avoid hailing unmetered street cabs; instead, use Uber or official airport taxi counters ('Sitio' taxis). Stay on well-lit main tourist streets after dark, drink only bottled or purified water (avoid tap water), and exercise caution with aggressive time-share salesmen at airport arrival halls in resort areas.
