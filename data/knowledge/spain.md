# Destination: Spain

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Schengen Short-Stay Visa (Type C) to travel to Spain for tourism. Applications must be lodged via BLS International Visa Services in India at least 15 to 45 working days before the scheduled departure. Mandatory requirements include a passport with 6 months validity, 30,000 EUR travel health insurance, confirmed flight itineraries, hotel bookings, past 3 months' bank statements certified by the bank, and income tax documents. The standard Schengen visa fee is 90 EUR for adults.

## Category: Transport
Spain features one of the most extensive high-speed rail networks in Europe (AVE operated by Renfe, along with budget competitors Ouigo and Iryo), connecting Madrid to Barcelona, Seville, and Valencia in 2.5 to 3 hours. Both Madrid (Metro de Madrid) and Barcelona (TMB Metro) have modern underground rail networks; purchasing a multi-trip transit card (e.g., T-Casual in Barcelona) saves money on local journeys. For airport transfers, express buses and commuter trains (Cercanías / Rodalies) provide quick links to city centers.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Euro (EUR). Contactless credit and debit card payments are ubiquitous across Spain, accepted by nearly all merchants, supermarkets, taxis, and tapas bars. Carrying a small amount of cash in 10 or 20 Euro notes is handy for small beach kiosks, local municipal markets (Mercado de San Miguel), or tipping. Tipping is not obligatory in Spain, though rounding up or leaving 5-10% in sit-down restaurants for good service is customary.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Spanish daily schedules are structured later than in other countries: lunch is typically eaten between 2:00 PM and 4:00 PM, while dinner rarely begins before 8:30 PM or 9:00 PM. Many smaller shops in provincial towns close for afternoon siesta between 2:00 PM and 5:00 PM. Greet shopkeepers with 'Hola' upon entering and 'Adiós' or 'Hasta luego' upon leaving. Respect local regional sensitivities in Catalonia and the Basque Country regarding language and identity.

## Category: Safety And Scams
While violent crime is extremely low, pickpocketing is common in crowded areas of Barcelona (Las Ramblas, Sagrada Familia, Barceloneta Beach) and Madrid (Puerta del Sol, Plaza Mayor, El Rastro market). Beware of distraction techniques, such as people asking for signatures on fake charity petitions or "spilling" liquids on you. Keep bags zippered, wear backpacks on your front inside metro wagons, and never leave phones unattended on restaurant tables.
