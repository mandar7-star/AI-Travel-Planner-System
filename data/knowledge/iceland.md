# Destination: Iceland

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Schengen Short-Stay Visa to visit Iceland. Visa applications are handled through VFS Global (often represented by the Embassy of Denmark or direct Icelandic missions in India) and must include a passport valid for six months, Schengen travel health insurance with minimum 30,000 EUR coverage, confirmed flight tickets, lodging bookings across Iceland, proof of sufficient financial funds, and employment documentation. Processing takes roughly 15 working days.

## Category: Transport
Iceland has no public railway network; transportation revolves around road travel and domestic flights. Renting a 4x4 vehicle or campervan is the most popular way to explore the famous Ring Road (Route 1) and the Golden Circle, allowing access to remote waterfalls and geothermal landscapes. For travellers who do not wish to drive, organized day coach tours (Flybus, Reykjavik Excursions) provide convenient pickups from Reykjavik to major attractions like the South Coast, Glacier Lagoon, and Blue Lagoon.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Icelandic Króna (ISK). Iceland is one of the most cashless nations on Earth, with credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) accepted everywhere, including unmanned fuel pumps, remote hot springs, public restrooms, and small roadside snack shacks. Carrying physical ISK currency is practically unnecessary throughout your journey. When refueling rental cars at automated gas stations, remember that a chip-and-PIN credit card is strictly required.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Icelandic culture is egalitarian, progressive, and deeply connected to nature and folklore. Before entering geothermal public swimming pools and lagoons (such as the Blue Lagoon or Sky Lagoon), you are legally required to shower thoroughly with soap naked in the communal shower facilities before putting on your swimwear. Never walk on delicate Icelandic volcanic moss, which takes decades to recover from human trampling, and never litter.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Iceland is ranked as the safest country in the world with almost zero crime. The primary hazards in Iceland are natural and weather-related: gale-force winds, sudden blizzards, black ice on roads, sneaker waves on Reynisfjara Black Sand Beach, and unstable glacier edges. Always monitor SafeTravel.is and Vedur.is for road condition updates and weather alerts before driving each day.
