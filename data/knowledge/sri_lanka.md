# Destination: Sri Lanka

## Category: Visa
Indian citizens traveling to Sri Lanka for tourism require an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) or online entry permit. Under bilateral tourism initiatives, visa fees are frequently waived or charged at a nominal fee for Indian tourists, though pre-registration through the official online portal remains mandatory. The standard tourist visa grants a stay of up to 30 days and requires a passport valid for at least six months from the arrival date, proof of confirmed return flights, and accommodation details.

## Category: Transport
Exploring Sri Lanka is best done by hiring a private air-conditioned car with an English-speaking driver, which is both convenient and surprisingly cost-effective for multi-day itineraries. Scenic train routes, particularly the iconic ride from Kandy to Ella through lush tea plantations, are a must-do highlight and should be booked well in advance. For short city trips in Colombo or Galle, PickMe and Uber apps provide reliable, metered three-wheeler (tuk-tuk) and car rides.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). Major hotels, upscale dining establishments, and national park ticketing counters accept international Visa and Mastercard credit cards. However, cash is essential for roadside tea stalls, fruit markets, tipping guides and drivers, and local tuk-tuks. Indian travellers can also benefit from UPI acceptance, which is being deployed across prominent merchant touchpoints in Sri Lanka.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Sri Lanka is primarily Buddhist, with significant Hindu, Muslim, and Christian heritage. When entering Buddhist temples (such as the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic in Kandy), wear white or light-colored clothing covering shoulders and knees, and remove footwear and headwear. Never pose for photos with your back turned to a Buddha statue, and refrain from having visible Buddha tattoos. The traditional greeting is 'Ayubowan', performed with pressed palms and a gentle head bow.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Sri Lanka is a welcoming and peaceful country for international tourists. Common tourist scams include fake gemstone sellers, unlicensed safari jeep operators at national parks, and inflated tuk-tuk fares outside major tourist hotels. Always book wildlife safaris through registered lodge partners and use the PickMe app for fair taxi pricing. Be cautious when swimming in ocean waters with strong undercurrents on southern beaches.
