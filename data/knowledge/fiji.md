# Destination: Fiji

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders can visit Fiji visa-free for up to 4 months for tourism purposes upon arrival at Nadi International Airport (NAN). To qualify for the entry permit on arrival, Indian citizens must present a passport valid for at least six months beyond the intended departure date, a confirmed return or onward air ticket, proof of sufficient funds for maintenance during the stay, and confirmed hotel or resort accommodation bookings. No visa application fee is charged upon entry.

## Category: Transport
Travel between Fiji's main island (Viti Levu) and the idyllic outer island groups (Mamanuca and Yasawa Islands) is conducted via fast catamarans operated by South Sea Cruises from Port Denarau, or via scenic island-hopper seaplanes and helicopters. On the mainland of Viti Levu, express buses connect Nadi, Suva, and the Coral Coast along the Queens Highway, while yellow metered taxis and private resort transfer shuttles are readily available. Renting a 4WD car allows travelers to explore scenic highlands like the Nausori Highlands.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Fijian Dollar (FJD / $). Major international resort properties, dive operators, licensed excursion companies, and restaurants accept Visa, Mastercard, and American Express (often with a standard 2.5% to 3.5% credit card administration surcharge). Carrying cash in Fijian Dollars is essential for visiting traditional rural Fijian villages, shopping at local municipal markets (such as Nadi Market), and taking local taxis. ATMs from ANZ, Westpac, and Bank of South Pacific (BSP) are available in Nadi, Suva, and Port Denarau.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Fiji is world-famous for its warm, joyful hospitality and the enthusiastic greeting of 'Bula!'. When visiting traditional Fijian villages, you should participate in the 'Sevusevu' customary protocol by presenting a gift of Kava root (waka) to the village chief. Inside indigenous villages, visitors must dress modestly (covering shoulders and wearing a sulu/sarong covering knees), remove hats and sunglasses from the head (which is considered disrespectful), and avoid touching anyone's head.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Fiji is a peaceful and safe destination with friendly local communities. Safety precautions involve exercising common-sense awareness in urban parts of Suva and Nadi after dark and avoiding unlicensed pirate taxis. In ocean waters, always be mindful of coral reef cuts, tidal currents, and sea urchins; wear water shoes when wading around shallow reef flats and only book marine charters through reputable, licensed operators with certified safety equipment.
