# Destination: Morocco

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders can apply for an online Morocco Electronic Travel Authorization (e-Visa) via the official Access Maroc platform (acces-maroc.ma). The e-Visa is available to Indian nationals holding a valid visa or permanent residency with at least 90 days validity from the Schengen Area, USA, UK, Canada, Japan, or Australia. The standard e-Visa fee is approx. 770 MAD (approx. 75 USD), taking 1 to 3 business days for electronic approval. Indian citizens without qualifying visas must apply through VFS Global Morocco in India.

## Category: Transport
Morocco possesses the best railway system in Africa, operated by ONCF, featuring the Al Boraq high-speed bullet train that connects Casablanca, Rabat, and Tangier in just over two hours. For reaching Marrakech, Fez, and Meknes, comfortable Atlas express trains run frequently. Inside imperial cities, small city taxis ('Petits Taxis') are metered, while grand taxis ('Grands Taxis') run fixed shared intercity routes. Within ancient labyrinthine medinas (like Marrakech and Fez), walking is the only viable mode of transport as motor vehicles are prohibited.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Moroccan Dirham (MAD), which is a closed currency that cannot be legally traded outside Morocco; withdraw MAD from official bank ATMs upon arrival (Attijariwafa Bank, BMCE). Credit cards are accepted in modern shopping malls, high-end Riads, and upscale restaurants, but physical cash is indispensable for souks, local street food stalls, medina handicraft shops, and hammams. Always keep small change (10, 20 MAD notes and coins) for tipping and street purchases.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Moroccan culture combines Berber, Arab, and French influences with deeply rooted Islamic traditions. Dress respectfully with shoulders and knees covered, particularly when navigating medinas and rural mountain villages. Always ask permission before photographing medina craftsmen, snake charmers, or water sellers in Jemaa el-Fnaa, who will expect a tip of 10-20 MAD. When eating traditional communal tagines or couscous with your hands, use only the right hand.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Morocco is generally safe for travelers, though navigating imperial medinas involves persistent street hustlers and faux guides. Common medina scams include youths falsely claiming that certain streets or squares are 'closed today' due to prayers and offering to guide you through alternate routes ending in high-pressure carpet or leather tannery shops. Use offline navigation apps like Google Maps or Maps.me, politely decline unsolicited street directions with 'La, Shukran' (No, thank you), and use official licensed city guides booked through your riad or local tourist office.
