# Destination: Seychelles

## Category: Visa
Seychelles is a visa-free country for all nationalities, including Indian citizens. Indian passport holders are granted a free Visitor's Permit valid for up to 30 days upon arrival at Seychelles International Airport (SEZ). Mandatory entry requirements include applying for the mandatory digital Seychelles Travel Authorization (TA) online at seychelles.govtas.com before departure (nominal processing fee applies), holding a passport valid for the duration of stay, confirmed return or onward flights, proof of prepaid accommodation at licensed tourism establishments, and proof of sufficient funds (minimum 150 USD per day).

## Category: Transport
Traveling between the main granitic islands (Mahé, Praslin, and La Digue) is done via Cat Cocos fast catamarans or quick 15-minute scheduled flights on Air Seychelles Twin Otters between Mahé and Praslin. On the main island of Mahé and on Praslin, renting an automatic compact car is the most practical and scenic way to explore secluded beaches, while public SPTC buses operate inexpensive fixed routes. On the car-free island of La Digue, renting a traditional bicycle is the primary and most enjoyable mode of transport.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Seychellois Rupee (SCR). Major international credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are accepted across hotels, resorts, car rental agencies, ferry ticket counters, and established sit-down restaurants. Carrying a small amount of Seychellois Rupee cash (and some Euro cash notes) is necessary for renting bicycles on La Digue, buying fresh tropical fruit at Victoria Market, dining at local Creole takeaway boxes (takeaways offer great value), and paying national park beach entrance fees (like Anse Source d'Argent).

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Seychellois culture is a harmonious blend of African Creole, French, British, and Asian heritage with a laid-back island rhythm. The national languages are Seychellois Creole (Seselwa), English, and French. Locals appreciate a polite greeting ('Bonzour' or 'Good morning') before starting conversations. When visiting nature reserves and marine parks (like Vallée de Mai UNESCO World Heritage Site or Aldabra), adhere strictly to environmental rules: never harvest endemic Coco de Mer nuts, disturb nesting sea turtles, or step on living corals.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Seychelles is a safe and peaceful destination with negligible violent crime. The main travel risks are related to natural ocean conditions: during monsoon transitions, certain beaches (like Grand Anse on La Digue or Anse Intendance on Mahé) experience strong undertows and dangerous rip currents where swimming is perilous; always observe beach warning flags. Exercise standard care by not leaving valuables unattended on remote beaches while snorkeling or swimming.
