# Destination: Argentina

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a tourist visa to visit Argentina. Indian citizens holding a valid, unexpired US Tourist Visa (B1/B2) or Schengen Visa can apply online for an Electronic Travel Authorization (AVE - Autorización de Viaje Electrónica) via the Argentine National Directorate of Migration for a fee of 200 USD, processed in 10 to 20 working days. Indian citizens without a valid US or Schengen visa must apply for a traditional sticker tourist visa at the Embassy of Argentina in New Delhi or the Consulate in Mumbai, requiring verified bank solvency documents, travel itineraries, and an in-person interview.

## Category: Transport
Argentina is the eighth-largest country in the world, making domestic flights on Aerolíneas Argentinas or Flybondi essential for traveling between Buenos Aires, Iguazú Falls, Mendoza wine country, and Patagonian hubs like Bariloche and El Calafate. In Buenos Aires, the underground Subte (subway) and extensive 'Colectivo' bus network operate with the reloadable SUBE contactless transit card. For city rides in Buenos Aires and Mendoza, ride-hailing apps like Uber and Cabify operate efficiently alongside metered black-and-yellow city radio taxis.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the Argentine Peso (ARS / $). Foreign credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) automatically benefit from the official MEP exchange rate (Mercado Electrónico de Pagos), giving tourists near-blue-market value for card swipes without the need to carry stacks of physical cash. International cards are accepted in hotels, wine bodegas, supermarkets, and restaurants. However, keeping some ARS cash is advisable for small tipping (propina standard is 10% in restaurants), small neighborhood kiosks (kioscos), and public transport card top-ups.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Argentine culture combines European heritage (principally Italian and Spanish) with passionate South American warmth, renowned for tango, world-class beef (asado), and Malbec wine. Greetings typically involve a warm single cheek kiss among men and women. Dining hours are very late: dinner rarely begins before 9:00 PM or 10:00 PM, and restaurants remain lively past midnight. Sharing mate (a traditional herbal infusion) from a communal gourd is a profound ritual of friendship; accept it gratefully and do not move the metal straw (bombilla).

## Category: Safety And Scams
Argentina is among the safer destinations in South America, but common urban petty theft occurs in Buenos Aires neighborhoods like La Boca (stick strictly to the tourist strip of El Caminito during daylight), San Telmo flea markets, and around major train terminals (Retiro and Constitución). Beware of the classic 'mustard scam' where a stranger squirts sauce onto your clothes and an accomplice offers to clean it while stealing your bag. Always keep your smartphone stored inside zipped bags rather than holding it loosely near busy roadways.
