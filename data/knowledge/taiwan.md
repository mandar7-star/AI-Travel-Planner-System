# Destination: Taiwan

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a tourist visa to visit Taiwan, obtainable via an online eVisa or through the Taipei Economic and Cultural Center (TECC). Indian citizens who possess a valid visa or permanent residency from the USA, Canada, Japan, UK, Schengen countries, Australia, or New Zealand can apply for a free Taiwan Travel Authorization Certificate (TAC) online, which grants 14 days of visa-free entry. Standard eVisa applications are processed within 3 to 5 business days with proof of onward travel and financial documentation.

## Category: Transport
Taiwan has one of the world's most convenient and reliable transit networks, anchored by the Taiwan High Speed Rail (THSR) connecting Taipei on the west coast all the way to Kaohsiung in just 90 minutes. Taipei and Kaohsiung feature immaculate MRT metro systems with clear English wayfinding and frequent trains. Get an EasyCard or iPASS at any metro station or convenience store to pay for subways, local buses, YouBike public bike shares, and railway connections seamlessly. Uber and yellow metered taxis operate reliably in all major cities.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The currency is the New Taiwan Dollar (NTD / TWD). Credit and debit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are widely accepted in department stores, hotels, train ticket counters, and established sit-down restaurants. Cash remains essential for visiting world-famous night markets (like Shilin, Raohe, and Fengjia), buying street snacks, and shopping at traditional tea stalls. You can easily withdraw NTD using international bank debit cards at 7-Eleven and FamilyMart convenience store ATMs throughout the country.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Taiwanese society is known for exceptional politeness, cleanliness, and community order. Eating, drinking, and chewing betel nut or gum are strictly forbidden inside MRT stations and train cars, carrying on-the-spot fines. Stand in orderly lines marked on metro platforms and let passengers disembark before boarding. When visiting Taoist or Buddhist temples, step over the raised wooden thresholds rather than standing on them, and speak in quiet, measured tones.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Taiwan ranks among the safest nations globally, with virtually non-existent street crime and violent theft. Tourists can walk safely in any neighborhood even late at night. The main precautions involve paying attention to scooter traffic when crossing pedestrian crossings, and checking weather forecasts for typhoon warnings or earthquake advisories during the summer months. Overcharging is rare, but verify that taxi drivers activate the meter before departing.
