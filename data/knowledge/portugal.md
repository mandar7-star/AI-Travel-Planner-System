# Destination: Portugal

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders require a Schengen Short-Stay Visa to visit Portugal for tourism. Applications are submitted via VFS Global Portugal in India with standard Schengen documentation: passport with at least six months validity, confirmed return flights, hotel vouchers across mainland Portugal and the Azores/Madeira, 30,000 EUR travel health insurance, salary slips, and certified 3-month bank statements. Standard visa processing time is approximately 15 working days.

## Category: Transport
Portugal has a convenient and affordable transit infrastructure. In Lisbon and Porto, metro systems, vintage wooden trams (such as Lisbon's famous Tram 28), funiculars, and suburban trains (CP - Comboios de Portugal) connect city sights, Sintra, Cascais, and beaches efficiently. For intercity travel between Lisbon, Porto, Coimbra, and Faro in the Algarve, CP Alfa Pendular high-speed trains provide comfortable journeys. Ride-hailing apps like Uber and Bolt are ubiquitous, fast, and relatively inexpensive across Portuguese cities.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Euro (EUR). Contactless credit and debit card payments are widely accepted throughout hotels, supermarkets, restaurants, and museum ticket desks. Some small traditional pastelarias (pastry shops), kiosks, and rural establishments may still require cash for small transactions under 5 or 10 EUR (look for the Multibanco sign). Avoid high-fee standalone tourist ATMs and use Multibanco ATMs affiliated with major banks (like Caixa Geral or Millennium BCP) for cash withdrawals.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Portuguese society is polite, hospitable, and welcoming. Greet locals with 'Bom dia' (good morning) or 'Boa tarde' (good afternoon) when entering shops and restaurants. When seated at a restaurant, servers will often place appetizers like bread, olives, butter, and cheese on your table; note that these are not complimentary and will be billed only if consumed. Portugal has a deep maritime and musical heritage, and quiet respect is appreciated during live Fado singing performances.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Portugal is regularly ranked among the top five safest countries in the world with minimal violent crime. The most frequent tourist annoyance is pickpocketing on crowded tourist tram lines (Tram 28 and Tram 15 in Lisbon) and around popular squares like Rossio and Praça do Comércio. Street hustlers in Lisbon may approach tourists offering fake spices disguised as illicit substances; ignore them completely and keep walking.
