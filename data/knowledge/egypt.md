# Destination: Egypt

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders can obtain an Egypt Tourist e-Visa online via the official Egypt e-Visa portal (visa2egypt.gov.eg) or apply for a Visa on Arrival if holding a valid, used visa or permanent residence permit for the US, UK, Schengen Area, Japan, or Canada. The single-entry e-Visa fee is 25 USD (or 60 USD for multiple-entry) and requires a passport valid for at least 6 months, confirmed hotel reservations, and return flight details. Travelers applying directly through the Egyptian Embassy in India without qualifying third-country visas require a standard paper application.

## Category: Transport
In Cairo, the underground Cairo Metro and ride-hailing apps like Uber and Careem are the safest, cheapest, and most convenient ways to travel while avoiding aggressive airport taxi negotiations. For intercity travel to Luxor, Aswan, and Alexandria, sleeper trains (Watania Sleeping Trains) and modern express day trains run along the Nile corridor, while EgyptAir operates quick domestic flights between Cairo, Hurghada, Sharm El Sheikh, and Luxor. A multi-day Nile River cruise between Luxor and Aswan remains an iconic mode of transport.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Egyptian Pound (EGP). Credit cards are accepted in international hotels, upscale restaurants, and official archaeological site ticketing counters (where card-only payments are legally enforced for monument entries). Cash is essential for tips ('baksheesh'), small street bazaars (Khan el-Khalili), felucca boat rides, and camel or horse carriage operators. Carry small denomination EGP banknotes (5, 10, 20, 50 EGP) as vendors frequently claim not to have change.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Egypt is a conservative Muslim-majority society rich in ancient and Islamic history. Modest clothing covering shoulders, cleavage, and knees is strongly recommended for men and women when exploring downtown streets, local markets, and religious monuments like the Mosque of Muhammad Ali. Tipping ('baksheesh') is an integral part of daily life and social expectations in Egypt; offering small tips to drivers, luggage handlers, restroom attendants, and temple guards is standard practice.

## Category: Safety And Scams
While violent crime against tourists is rare and tourist police are present at all major sites, aggressive touts and scams are rampant around the Giza Pyramids and Luxor temples. Common scams include touts falsely claiming ticket offices have moved, forcing camel rides at inflated prices, demanding payment for pointing at ruins, or handing you souvenirs as 'gifts' before demanding money. Always buy official entrance tickets directly from government ticket counters, agree on all excursion prices upfront, and firmly say 'La, Shukran' (No, thank you) to unwanted solicitations.
