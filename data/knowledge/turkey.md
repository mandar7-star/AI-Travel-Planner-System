# Destination: Turkey

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders holding a valid, active Schengen, USA, UK, or Ireland visa or residence permit are eligible to apply for a single-entry 30-day Turkey e-Visa online via the official Republic of Türkiye electronic visa portal (evisa.gov.tr) for a fee of approx. 50-60 USD, processed immediately. Indian citizens without one of these supporting visas must apply for a standard sticker tourist visa through Gateway Globe visa centers in India with proof of funds, hotel bookings, flight tickets, and travel insurance, which takes 7 to 15 working days.

## Category: Transport
Istanbul has an extensive public transportation network comprising the Metro, historic and modern Trams (e.g., T1 tram line connecting Sultanahmet to Eminönü and Kabataş), Marmaray cross-continental undersea rail, and Bosphorus commuter ferries. Purchase an Istanbulkart at airport transit stations or tram stops and top it up with cash or credit card for discounted fares across all city transit modes. For domestic intercity travel between Istanbul, Cappadocia, Antalya, and Izmir, Turkish Airlines and Pegasus Airlines operate frequent, economical domestic flights.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Turkish Lira (TRY / ₺). Contactless credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are accepted across almost all major retail chains, hotels, supermarkets, and restaurants. However, physical cash in Turkish Lira is essential for purchasing street foods (simit, roasted chestnuts), riding shared dolmuş minibuses, visiting local bazaars, and tipping. Due to inflation fluctuations, avoid carrying excessive leftover TRY cash at the end of your holiday.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Turkey is a transcontinental nation with deep Islamic traditions and warm Mediterranean hospitality. When visiting active mosques like the Blue Mosque or Hagia Sophia, women must cover their heads with a scarf and cover shoulders and knees, while men must wear pants covering knees; shoes must be removed before stepping onto mosque carpets. Accepting tea (çay) offered by shopkeepers in the Grand Bazaar is a polite social gesture, even if you do not end up making a purchase.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Turkey is generally welcoming and safe for tourists, though common scams occur in Istanbul around Sultanahmet, Taksim, and Istiklal Street. Beware of the infamous "let's have a drink" bar scam where friendly strangers invite tourists to a nightclub resulting in extortionate bills, and the dropped shoeshine brush scam where a shoeshiner demands high payment after pretending to offer a free shine. In taxis, always ensure the meter is started, or book rides via the BiTaksi app to prevent manipulated fares.
