# Destination: Croatia

## Category: Visa
Croatia is a full member of the Schengen Area, meaning Indian citizens require a Schengen Short-Stay Visa to visit Dubrovnik, Split, Zagreb, and the Dalmatian islands. Applications must be lodged through VFS Global Croatia in India. Mandatory documents include a valid passport, minimum 30,000 EUR travel health insurance, confirmed flight itineraries, verified hotel bookings, 3-6 months bank statements, and income tax returns. Processing time is generally 15 calendar days.

## Category: Transport
Getting around Croatia is primarily done by express buses (such as Arriva and FlixBus) for overland routes between Zagreb, Plitvice Lakes, and coastal cities, as train connections in the south are limited. Coastal navigation and island-hopping between Split, Hvar, Korčula, and Dubrovnik are efficiently handled by Jadrolinija and Kapetan Luka (Krilo) catamarans and ferries. In Dubrovnik and Split, city buses operate regularly, while old town pedestrian zones are strictly car-free.

## Category: Currency And Payments
Croatia adopted the Euro (EUR) as its official currency. Credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are accepted across hotels, national park entrances, modern restaurants, and ferry ticket offices. Having 20 to 50 EUR cash is beneficial for buying produce at open-air green markets, tipping, paying for local boat transfers, and visiting small bakeries (pekara). Always choose Croatian bank ATMs (such as Zagrebačka banka or PBZ) to withdraw cash and decline DCC (dynamic currency conversion) markups.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Croatians possess a vibrant cafe culture, often spending hours sipping coffee ('kava') on sunny waterfront promenades (Riva). Dress respectably when entering historic churches, cathedrals, and monasteries, ensuring shoulders and knees are covered. In coastal towns like Dubrovnik, walking around the historic old town streets wearing only swimwear is prohibited by municipal bylaws and punishable by steep fines.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Croatia is one of the safest countries in Europe with exceptionally low street crime rates. In tourist-dense areas like Dubrovnik Old Town and Split Diocletian's Palace, watch out for overpriced unauthorized water taxis and exchange booths offering poor rates. Always confirm taxi fares via apps like Uber or Bolt, or ensure the taximeter is active before departing from airports or ferry terminals.
