# Destination: Peru

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders holding a valid, unexpired visa or permanent residency from the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, Japan, or the Schengen Area (with minimum 6 months validity remaining) are exempt from visa requirements and can enter Peru visa-free for tourism for up to 183 days. Indian passport holders who do not hold any of these qualifying third-country visas must apply for a standard tourist visa in person at the Embassy of Peru in New Delhi or the Consulate in Mumbai, providing return flight bookings, hotel reservations, 3 months certified bank statements, and employment proof.

## Category: Transport
Due to the dramatic Andes mountain geography, domestic flights (LATAM, Sky Airline) are the fastest way to travel from Lima to Cusco (gateway to Machu Picchu), Arequipa, and the Amazon (Iquitos/Puerto Maldonado). To reach Machu Picchu from Cusco or Ollantaytambo, scenic trains operated by PeruRail or Inca Rail are mandatory and must be booked months in advance. In Lima, the Metropolitano dedicated bus rapid transit system links Miraflores and the historic city center, while Uber and Cabify offer safe, reliable, and affordable point-to-point urban rides.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Peruvian Sol (PEN / S/.). Credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are widely accepted across Lima, Cusco, and the Sacred Valley in tourist hotels, travel agencies, supermarkets, and upscale restaurants. Physical cash in Peruvian Soles (as well as crisp, unblemished US Dollar bills) is essential for local artisan markets (like Pisac Market), tipping trekking porters and mountain guides, and paying small archaeological entry fees. Use bank-attached ATMs (BCP, BBVA, Interbank) to withdraw cash securely.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Peru is a culturally fascinating country blending ancient Inca indigenous customs with Spanish colonial traditions. When visiting Andean indigenous communities in Cusco or Lake Titicaca, ask permission before photographing locals wearing traditional Andean dress (offering a small tip of 2 to 5 Soles is customary). Respect ancient ruins and sacred mountain deities ('Apus') by not climbing on fragile Incan stone walls. Tipping in sit-down restaurants is customary at 10% for good service.

## Category: Safety And Scams
The main tourist corridor of Peru (Lima's Miraflores and Barranco, Cusco, Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu) is well-policed and generally safe. The primary health concern is high-altitude sickness (soroche) in Cusco (3,400m) and Lake Titicaca (3,800m); take it easy on your first day, stay hydrated, and drink local coca tea or take altitude medication. Beware of unmetered street taxis at Lima's Jorge Chávez International Airport (always use official airport taxi desks inside the arrivals hall or Uber) and verify that trekking operators on the Inca Trail are officially licensed.
