# Destination: Mauritius

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders traveling to Mauritius for tourism are granted a free 60-day Visa on Arrival at Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport (MRU). Requirements for entry include a passport valid for at least six months from the date of arrival, a confirmed return air ticket, a confirmed hotel or accommodation booking voucher, and proof of sufficient funds (a minimum of 100 USD per day of stay or valid international credit cards). All arriving passengers must fill out the online Mauritius All-in-One Travel Digital Form prior to boarding.

## Category: Transport
Mauritius is best explored by renting a car or hiring a private air-conditioned car with a driver for full-day excursions (covering North tour, South tour, and Chamarel). Driving is on the left side of the road, matching Indian driving rules. In urban corridors between Port Louis, Rose Hill, and Curepipe, the modern Metro Express light rail provides fast, air-conditioned public transit. For reaching beautiful offshore islets like Île aux Cerfs or Îlot Gabriel, daily catamaran cruises and speedboat tours operate from coastal jetties.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Mauritian Rupee (MUR / Rs). Major international credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are widely accepted across resorts, boutique hotels, shopping complexes (such as Bagatelle Mall and Caudan Waterfront), supermarkets, and dive centers. Carrying Mauritian Rupee cash is necessary for buying street snacks (like Dholl Puri and Gateaux Piments), shopping at Central Market in Port Louis, and tipping local tour guides. Official bank ATMs (MCB, SBM) are widespread across the island.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Mauritius is a peaceful multicultural island with a substantial population of Indian diaspora heritage, along with Creole, Franco-Mauritian, and Sino-Mauritian communities. Respectful and polite behavior is valued across all religious temples, mosques, and churches (such as the sacred Ganga Talao / Grand Bassin lake and temple complex), where shoes must be removed before entering and modest clothing covering shoulders and knees is mandatory. English and French are official languages, while Mauritian Creole is the daily lingua franca.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Mauritius is among the safest travel destinations in Africa with very low crime rates. Standard precautions against opportunistic petty theft should be taken on public beaches (do not leave phones and wallets unattended on beach towels while swimming) and in busy Port Louis markets. Avoid aggressive beach hawkers selling unregulated water sports or unlicensed catamaran trips; instead, book marine excursions through registered nautical operators or your hotel activity desk.
