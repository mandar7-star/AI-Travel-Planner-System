# Destination: Georgia

## Category: Visa
Indian passport holders holding a valid visa or permanent residence permit from the USA, UK, Canada, Schengen Area member states, Japan, Australia, New Zealand, or GCC countries are eligible to enter Georgia visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Indian citizens without a valid visa from these qualifying jurisdictions must apply for an e-Visa through the official Georgian e-Visa portal (evisa.gov.ge) or through the Embassy of Georgia in New Delhi. The e-Visa fee is 20 USD (plus a 2% service fee) and requires a passport valid for at least 3 months beyond the intended stay, return tickets, travel insurance, hotel reservations, and 3 months bank statements.

## Category: Transport
In the capital city of Tbilisi, getting around is easy with the Tbilisi Metro and modern municipal bus network using the Metromoney contactless transit card, while the aerial cable car to Narikala Fortress offers dramatic city views. For intercity travel to the Black Sea resort of Batumi or cultural hubs like Kutaisi, modern Stadler double-decker express trains operated by Georgian Railway offer fast, comfortable rides. For reaching high Caucasus mountain villages like Kazbegi (Stepantsminda) or Mestia (Svaneti), shared 'marshrutka' minibuses or private 4WD hire are standard. Ride-hailing apps like Bolt and Yandex Go operate reliably in all major cities.

## Category: Currency And Payments
The official currency is the Georgian Lari (GEL / ₾). Contactless credit and debit cards (Visa/Mastercard) and Apple Pay/Google Pay are accepted across hotels, supermarkets, restaurants, and wine bars in Tbilisi and Batumi. Cash in Georgian Lari is essential for traditional open-air bazaars (like Dezerter Bazaar), roadside churchkhela stalls, rural mountain guesthouses, and marshrutka minibus fares. ATMs (TBC Bank, Bank of Georgia) are widely available in towns for cash withdrawals with low conversion fees.

## Category: Culture And Etiquette
Georgia is considered the birthplace of wine, with an 8,000-year-old winemaking tradition (qvevri method) and an ancient Orthodox Christian heritage. Hospitality is sacred, expressed through the famous 'Supra' traditional feast led by a toastmaster ('Tamada'). When visiting historic Orthodox churches and monasteries (such as Holy Trinity Cathedral / Sameba or Gergeti Trinity Church), women must cover their heads with a scarf and wear skirts covering knees (wrap skirts are provided at entrances), while men must wear long trousers and remove hats.

## Category: Safety And Scams
Georgia is ranked among the safest countries in Europe with very low crime rates. In tourist-heavy areas of Old Tbilisi (Shardeni Street, Meidan Square), be cautious of pushy restaurant touts and bars overcharging for drinks. When taking street taxis that are not booked via the Bolt app, always negotiate and agree on the fare before stepping into the vehicle. On mountain hiking routes in Svaneti and Kazbegi, check weather forecasts and mountain trail conditions before trekking.
